# Cover Letter Style Guide

## Tone and Voice

- Professional but conversational — avoid corporate clichés ("leverage", "synergy", "passionate about", "strongly resonates").
- Direct and specific — name the company's actual work, not generic praise.
- Confident without being boastful.
- First person. Not stiff or corporate.

## Structure

1. **Opening paragraph**: Introduce who the applicant is and what genuinely draws him to this role, then connect that to something specific and concrete about the company. Do NOT open with the company's name or mission as the first subject — the reader should feel introduced to a person, not confronted with a mirror of their own job ad.
2. **Middle paragraphs (1–2)**: Most relevant experience. Connect past achievements to the job's requirements. Use numbers where they appear in the CV — do not invent metrics.
3. **Closing paragraph**: Brief, genuine statement of enthusiasm and an invitation to talk. Keep it short — one or two sentences. No hollow phrases.

## Fabrication — absolute prohibition

Do not invent anything that cannot be traced to a specific line in the CV or experience notes:

- No job titles he has not held
- No tools, frameworks, or technologies not mentioned in the CV
- No projects, outcomes, or metrics not stated in the CV
- No responsibilities or team sizes not stated in the CV

If something relevant seems implied but is not explicitly stated, omit it or hedge it clearly.

## Knowledge vs. experience

When the CV shows adjacent exposure or theoretical familiarity rather than direct hands-on work, reflect this accurately. Use phrases like:

- "a solid grounding in X through my work on Y"
- "exposure to X in the context of Y"
- "familiarity with X, though my direct hands-on work has been in Y"

Do not write "I have experience with X" unless X is explicitly in the CV.

## Language

Write in the applicant's own framing. Do not mirror or paraphrase the job ad's specific vocabulary. Translate requirements into what he has actually done — not the other way round.

## Things to avoid

- Starting with "I am writing to apply for…"
- Opening with the company name as the first subject
- Repeating the CV verbatim — add context, not just facts
- Overly long letters — aim for one page when compiled
- Mirroring the job ad's phrasing or lifting its keywords verbatim
